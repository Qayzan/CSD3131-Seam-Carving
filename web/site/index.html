How it works

  1. Energy. Every pixel gets an energy: how strongly the brightness changes around it (3 × 3 Sobel filter, |Gx| + |Gy|). Edges are bright in the energy map, flat sky is dark.
  2. Find a seam. A vertical seam is one pixel per row, each next to the one above. Dynamic programming finds the seam with the lowest total energy by filling a table row by row. Greedy just takes the cheapest neighbour each row, which is fast but not always best. Graph cut turns the picture into a flow network and finds the minimum cut.
  3. Remove and repeat. The seam is removed, the energy is recalculated, and the next seam is found, until the picture reaches the target size. Horizontal seams work the same way on the transposed picture.

The page runs the team’s actual C++ code, compiled to WebAssembly. Nothing is uploaded: your picture never leaves your browser.
